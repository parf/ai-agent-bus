# TODO 0.6.0 — record kinds

📌 **TL;DR:** `kind` becomes a closed set of five, `mode` retires into it, a
service is an external one that is not on the bus, and 📡 carries secrets in a
field of their own. No migration is written: the records on the live node are
registered again with their kind.

## Objective

Close `kind` so the daemon answers what a record is instead of a page guessing
it. The design was settled with the owner in discussion; this file holds the
unfinished work and its acceptance. Substance moves to the owning `docs/`
section as each row lands, and decision names to
[decisions](../../docs/decisions.md#settled).

There is no compatibility obligation before 1.1, so **no migration is written**:
the seven records on the live node are registered again with their kind.

## The enum

| Wire value | Glyph | What it is |
|---|---|---|
| `user` | 👤 | the queue a person reads |
| `agent` | 👾 | the queue an agent reads |
| `queue` | 📮 | a registered queue, named for its own sake |
| `pubsub` | 📣 | a pub/sub channel; keeps nothing, copies to subscribers |
| `service` | 📡 | external, not on this bus; nothing is delivered |

**This plan was taxonomic only**, and the owner's Q78 answer added the one
thing the kind does enforce: a 📡 has no queue here, so send, subscribe and
consume refuse it after the ACL has been asked
([decision](../../docs/decisions.md#settled), built in 0.6.5). Nothing else
follows from the kind. One allow list still governs both directions, and
`ConsumeAs` still lets an admitted caller drain a record named after somebody
else. Splitting that list is [Q74](../R2.0/acl-direction.md#where-direction-is-needed)
and **no row here implements any part of it**.

Wire spellings are the lowercase forms already used by `topic` and `agent`;
`service` is fixed by the owner. Say the word and any of the other four change
before J.1 lands — afterwards it is a wire edit.

`generic` and `topic` disappear. ⚙️ stops meaning a record and stays with the
daemon (`agent-busd`, `docs/user/`).

## Remaining work

| ID | Deliverable | Depends on | Acceptance and mutation |
|---|---|---|---|
| J.1 | `kind` is a closed set | — | **Done in 0.6.3.** `register --kind banana` is refused, naming the five. Each of the five registers. Drop the validation and the `banana` check fails. Restore is J.11 and is not covered here |
| J.2 | `mode` retires | J.1 | **Done in 0.6.3.** `topic create --kind pubsub` is gone; 📮 and 📣 are registered by kind alone. No stored record carries `mode`, and the field leaves the wire. Leave `mode` populated on a 📣 and a check that the field is absent from `ls -j` fails |
| J.3 | `register` defaults to `service`, a script is an `agent` | J.1 | **Done in 0.6.3.** `register x@h --addr a --protocol p` with no `--kind` stores `service`. `agent-bus start` stores `agent` (`start.go:80`), and the started script still serves its inbox. Revert either literal separately and its own check fails — the two are independent edits, not one |
| J.4 | 📡 requires an address | J.1, J.3 | **Done in 0.6.3.** `register x@h` bare is refused; with `--addr` and `--protocol` it succeeds. Editing an existing 📡 to clear `protocol` **and separately to clear `addr`** is refused, not silently accepted, each with its own check and mutant — the check lives where `validatePersonal` lives, on the final record. Remove the edit-path check and the register check still passes while the edit check fails |
| J.5 | `--personal` is `agent` + flag | J.1 | **Done in 0.6.3.** A personal record is `agent`; `Personal` on any other kind is refused. Allow on a personal agent admits only other agents, and refuses a user, a group, `@owner` and `*`. Each forbidden assignment class has its own check &mdash; a user, a group, `@owner`, `*`, self and a non-empty Maintainers list &mdash; beside the positive case of another agent. `Personal` is refused on each of the other four kinds, not only on one. Restore the old `"generic"` literal and the personal-agent check fails; drop any one forbidden class and its own check fails |
| J.6 | 📡 holds secrets | J.1, J.4 | **Done in 0.6.8**, and owned by [services § secrets](../../docs/06-services.md#secrets). Q77 settled 2026-09-19: opaque bytes, `KEY=value` by convention, nothing parsed. A **new field, not [Config](../../docs/03-records.md#configuring-a-template)**: opaque text on a `service`, where Config is JSON on a template and stays readable by its own record alone. A secret is returned to whoever the record's ACL admits and refused to everyone else, with no second list; the secret **body** appears in no listing, no record answer, no page and no log, while the digest is present in each. A secret survives a restart, and a write is acknowledged only once it is durable. Every surface is pinned separately: `ls -j` and the record endpoint carry the digest **and** lack the bytes, the web detail redacts, and the daemon log never contains them. Remove the read check and the refusal check fails; return the bytes from `Public()` and the listing check fails; skip the checkpoint and the restart check fails; store a secret on any kind but `service` and that check fails |
| J.7 | Faces say what the enum says | J.1–J.5 | **Done in 0.6.3.** One listing per thing a record is: `/agents` (👾, first in the menu), `/services` (📡 alone), `/channels` (📮 📣 👤). `/personal` is a view of the agents, because Personal is agent-only. The record page is reached as `/agent`, `/service` or `/channel` and titles itself from the kind. ⚙️ appears on no record and stays with the daemon. Point `listPathFor` at one page for two kinds and the section-navigation check fails |
| J.10 | The target's own user state is asked only where it exists | J.1 | **Done in 0.6.3.** `suspension(to)` asks `active(r.Name)` of a `user` and an `agent` and of nothing else; a `queue`, a `pubsub` and a `service` are sent to without consulting a user state they cannot have. Positive checks for `user` and `agent` where the state exists, negatives for `queue`, `pubsub` and `service` where it cannot, and owner suspension asserted across all five, since it is unchanged. Ask the own-state question of a `queue` and that check fails; drop it for an `agent` and that check fails |
| J.11 | Restore has a stated rule for a kind it does not know | J.1 | **Done in 0.6.3.** A snapshot holding `generic` or `topic` is not silently accepted: restore refuses with a message naming the record and the kind, and the daemon does not come up pretending the state is current. Accept an unknown kind at restore and this check fails; refuse at register only and it still fails. Closing `kind` without this leaves a daemon that boots on state it cannot describe |
| J.12 | Cutover runbook for the live node | J.11 | **Done in 0.6.9**, and owned by the [cutover runbook](cutover.md#the-procedure); [setup § record kind upgrade](../../docs/09-setup.md#record-kind-upgrade) says what changes and points at it. **The procedure edits the stopped daemon's snapshot instead of re-registering**, because re-registration is the one operation that overwrites an allow list — the property this row most wants kept — and a queue lives in the snapshot beside its record either way. **Owner, ACL, Maintainers, Personal, Config and queued work** are each checked separately and the order relative to the incompatible restart is stated. Verified against a snapshot written in the pre-enum shape rather than a copy of the live one, because [the live snapshot](cutover.md#the-live-node-2026-09-19) holds nothing this version refuses: the fixture carries both topic modes, both `generic` cases, a person's own inbox, Personal, Maintainers, a configuration and two queues holding messages. Dropping any one of the six from the transform fails that property's own check. No compatibility obligation removes the migration *feature*; it does not remove the upgrade problem |
| J.13 | Execute the cutover on the live node | J.1–J.7, J.10–J.12 | **Done 2026-09-19**, on the owner’s instruction and by the [runbook](cutover.md#the-live-node-2026-09-19). The node came up on 0.6.9 with eleven records — seven 👾 and the four person inboxes now 👤 — and **no difference** in Owner, ACL, Maintainers, Personal, Config or queued work; the counters came across too. Six of the seven faces reconnected by themselves; `opencode/oab@parf.us` waits on its launcher |
| J.14 | The compatibility migrations go | J.11 | **Done in 0.6.3.** `administrator_migration.go` renames `@maintainers` and parks a colliding group as `@administrators-legacy`; `EstablishDaemonOwner` and `EstablishAccounts` seed a "legacy or first" snapshot; the daemon accepts a pre-0.5.62 string where an array is now written. **No compatibility obligation before 1.1 makes all of it dead weight**, and J.11 already states what restore does with state it cannot read. Remove each, and with it the four `legacy` paragraphs in [setup](../../docs/09-setup.md) and [identity](../../docs/01-identity-and-roles.md) that are their only reason to exist. Restore any one migration and the check that the daemon refuses old state instead of converting it fails |
| J.8 | Docs own the new words | J.1–J.7, J.10–J.14 | **Done in 0.6.5.** The page splits: [records](../../docs/03-records.md#how-to-call-it) keeps kinds, agent templates, configuration and topics, and [services](../../docs/06-services.md#what-a-service-is) owns the external 📡 case — its address, protocol, secrets and every refusal that follows from having no queue. Glossary, [discovery](../../docs/05-discovery.md) and [glyphs](web-handoff/glyphs.md) name five kinds and no `generic`. Decision rows link the owning section and do not restate it. A sweep leaves no `generic`, no `mode` and no stale `📥`/`⚙️` as a record label anywhere outside history, and every anchor a moved heading breaks is repointed. `#service-kinds` is repointed at `#record-kinds` outside history and `#service-and-template` becomes `#agent-templates`, the CLI verb following it to `agent-template`. **`#script-services` and `#services` kept their anchors here and were renamed in 0.6.9** on the owner's instruction, once counting showed nineteen inbound links rather than the fifty that bought the exception: they are `#script-agents` and `#record-authority`, repointed everywhere outside history |
