- ✅ 🟠 J16 `agent-bus call` under a name whose inbox a live face reads can lose its own reply to that face
  - Closed: `0212421a`, 0.9.138; verified in main history.
  - Evidence: 2026-10-28 21:55, `agent-bus call '#runner/parf@parf.us' --status` as `#claude/ab-dvp-b` printed nothing; the answer (`ok: 3/3 running …`, tag `64c462549774`) was delivered to the owner's kitty window, whose face holds that inbox. Earlier identical calls returned normally, so it is a race
  - Impact: any session that also runs the CLI under its own name; the call looks empty or times out while the answer lands in the chat
  > 💡👾 A CLI `call` waits on a reply route of its own (a private reply inbox, or a topic and tag the face never takes), so a face's unfiltered consume cannot take it.
  >
  > 💬👾 plan (AB, 2026-10-28), for review before any code. Reading `Bus.ConsumeAddressed` and `callTarget`: a filtered wait beats an unfiltered one only while it is outstanding. The CLI sends first and starts its filtered consume afterwards, and starts a new one after every receipt, so a reply (or the receipt) that arrives in either gap is taken by the face. `docs/04-messaging.md#one-reader-per-inbox` already names the receipt gap.
  > - Cause 1: the gap between `POST /send` and the first filtered consume. A fast agent (`#runner status`) answers inside it.
  > - Cause 2: the gap after a receipt, before the next filtered consume.
  > - Option A, CLI only: start the filtered consume before the send and keep two overlapping, so one is always outstanding. The client cannot know when the daemon has registered a wait, so cause 1 is narrowed, not closed. Not enough alone.
  > - Option B 🌟, daemon: a send that states a `wait` already tells the daemon the sender will read the reply. For that wait (sender inbox, topic and tag, until the deadline) a message matching topic and tag is offered only to filtered readers and otherwise stays queued; the unfiltered reader takes it after the deadline. Both gaps close, no client change, no new field. Cost: a duplicate or late reply to a call whose caller has gone reaches the face up to one wait later. Touches `core/bus.go` Send and Consume; the daemon must be restarted to take it, which is the owner's call.
  > - Test that fails on current code: a registered caller with a live unfiltered reader sends with a wait; the answer is sent at once, before any filtered consume starts; the filtered consume must get it (today the unfiltered reader does). A second case: an `ack` receipt then the answer, with the unfiltered reader waiting throughout. A third: after the deadline the unfiltered reader takes a matching leftover. Plain unfiltered traffic and a send without a wait are unchanged.
  > - Mutant (delivery): drop the reservation check on the consume path; the first case must fail on an assertion. Also: reserve past the deadline; reserve without matching the tag.
  > - Gate `--normal` under the lock; docs: `04-messaging.md` one-reader and call sections; version bump and changelog.
  > 💬👾 after review by #claude/ab2-review (these supersede option B above where they differ):
  > - Trigger (M2): not every send that sets a wait. The reservation is made only when a send with a wait names its own reply route: `reply_to` naming the sender itself with a topic and tag. The CLI `call` sets it (its 12-hex tag is already unique); MCP, web and client sends that read replies through an unfiltered reader are unaffected. The key is the sender's own inbox, that topic and that tag.
  > - Whose inbox (H2): only the sender's own. A `reply_to` naming a third party makes none. A cap on reservations per inbox and on how long one may last; above either the send behaves as today, it is not refused.
  > - What is held (M1): a reply to that request only, and the reservation ends at the first non-receipt match, so an ack and one answer are held and a duplicate or a new message on the same topic and tag flows to the face. A third sender's message on the topic and tag is not held.
  > - Release (H1): a timer per reservation. When it fires, matching queued messages are offered to a blocked unfiltered reader. Lazy expiry alone would leave a held reply stuck on a quiet inbox.
  > - Paths: held on both the delivery path and the queue scan, since a face that starts its read after the reply is queued must not take it; fanout copies and forwarding are untouched; the hold counts toward the inbox bound and the TTL, so a short-TTL reply may expire held; reservations live in memory and a daemon restart returns to today's behaviour.
  > - Docs: the request-and-reply and one-reader sections and the envelope comment say the bus never drops or refuses, and may hold a reply for the caller's own filtered reader until the deadline.
  > - Tests and mutants added: a third party's reply_to makes no hold; a different-tag call and a pool of two share readers are unaffected; the timer re-offer (a blocked unfiltered reader, no further sends); the queue-scan path; a second reply after the answer flows to the face. Mutants: no re-offer, reserve the wrong inbox, ignore the tag, ignore the topic, skip the queue-scan check.
